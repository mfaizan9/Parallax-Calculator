Diagram, not to scale. At the left is the Earth's orbit, drawn as a circle 1 astronomical unit across. Two lines of sight leave opposite sides of the orbit, cross at a nearby star, and continue to the celestial sphere drawn as an arc at the right. The half angle between the lines of sight, labelled pi, is the parallax angle; it is marked once at the star and once out on the celestial sphere. A brace above the diagram labels the distance d from the Earth's orbit to the star. A brace at the far right marks the star's apparent displacement on the celestial sphere. The diagram does not change as you use the calculator.
